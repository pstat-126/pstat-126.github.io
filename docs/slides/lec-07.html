Confidence Intervals for Mean Response and Prediction

Two Forecasting Questions

After fitting \(\widehat{Y} = \widehat{\beta}_0 + \widehat{\beta}_1 X\), we often want to say something about a new input value \(x^*\).

There are two fundamentally different questions:

Question 1 — Mean response: > “What is the average fuel efficiency for all cars weighing 3,500 lbs?”

Targets \(E(Y \mid X = x^*) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x^*\) — a fixed (unknown) parameter.

\(\longrightarrow\) Confidence interval for mean response

Question 2 — New individual observation: > “How many mpg will this specific 3,500 lb car get?”

Targets \(Y^* = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x^* + \varepsilon^*\) — a random variable with its own noise.

\(\longrightarrow\) Prediction interval

Both are centered at the same point estimate \(\widehat{Y}^* = \widehat{\beta}_0 + \widehat{\beta}_1 x^*\). They differ in what uncertainty they account for.

Variance of the Fitted Value \(\widehat{Y}^*\)

Our point estimate at \(x^*\) is \(\widehat{Y}^* = \widehat{\beta}_0 + \widehat{\beta}_1 x^*\). It carries uncertainty from both coefficient estimates.

Since \(\widehat{\beta}_0\) and \(\widehat{\beta}_1\) share the same data, they are correlated: \(\text{Cov}(\widehat{\beta}_0, \widehat{\beta}_1) = -\bar{X}\sigma^2/S_{xx}\).

Expanding the variance of \(\widehat{\beta}_0 + \widehat{\beta}_1 x^*\) and substituting this covariance:

\[\text{Var}(\widehat{Y}^*) = \sigma^2\!\left(\frac{1}{n} + \frac{(x^* - \bar{X})^2}{S_{xx}}\right)\]

Two things to notice:

  • Smallest at \(x^* = \bar{X}\) — we’re most certain about the fitted value at the center of the data
  • Grows as \(x^*\) moves away from \(\bar{X}\) — extrapolation compounds uncertainty

Confidence Interval for Mean Response

Replacing \(\sigma^2\) with MSE gives a \(t_{n-2}\) distribution:

\[\frac{\widehat{Y}^* - (\beta_0 + \beta_1 x^*)}{\left[\text{MSE}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{n} + \dfrac{(x^*-\bar{X})^2}{S_{xx}}\right)\right]^{1/2}} \sim t_{n-2}\]

A \(100(1-\alpha)\%\) confidence interval for \(E(Y \mid X = x^*)\):

\[\boxed{\widehat{Y}^* \;\pm\; t_{1-\alpha/2,\, n-2}\left[\text{MSE}\!\left(\frac{1}{n} + \frac{(x^*-\bar{X})^2}{S_{xx}}\right)\right]^{1/2}}\]

Two Sources of Uncertainty in Prediction

For a new observation \(Y^* = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x^* + \varepsilon^*\), the prediction error decomposes as:

\[Y^* - \widehat{Y}^* = \underbrace{\left[(\beta_0 + \beta_1 x^*) - \widehat{Y}^*\right]}_{\text{estimation error}} + \underbrace{\varepsilon^*}_{\text{new individual noise}}\]

These are independent, so their variances add:

Source Variance
Estimation error in \(\widehat{Y}^*\) \(\sigma^2\!\left(\dfrac{1}{n} + \dfrac{(x^*-\bar{X})^2}{S_{xx}}\right)\)
New individual noise \(\varepsilon^*\) \(\sigma^2\)
Total \(\sigma^2\!\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{n} + \dfrac{(x^*-\bar{X})^2}{S_{xx}}\right)\)

The extra \(\sigma^2\) is irreducible — it exists regardless of how much data we collect.

Prediction Interval

Replacing \(\sigma^2\) with MSE gives the sampling distribution of the prediction error:

\[\frac{Y^* - \widehat{Y}^*}{\left[\text{MSE}\!\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{n} + \dfrac{(x^*-\bar{X})^2}{S_{xx}}\right)\right]^{1/2}} \sim t_{n-2}\]

A \(100(1-\alpha)\%\) prediction interval for a new \(Y^*\) at \(X = x^*\):

\[\boxed{\widehat{Y}^* \;\pm\; t_{1-\alpha/2,\, n-2}\left[\text{MSE}\!\left(1 + \frac{1}{n} + \frac{(x^*-\bar{X})^2}{S_{xx}}\right)\right]^{1/2}}\]

The only difference from the CI is the \(+\,1\) inside the brackets.

Key consequence: Even as \(n \to \infty\), the PI does not collapse to a point:

\[\text{PI width} \to 2\,t \cdot \text{MSE}^{1/2} \quad \text{as } n \to \infty\]

There will always be irreducible noise in a new individual observation.

CI vs. PI: Side by Side

Confidence Interval Prediction Interval
Targets \(E(Y \mid X = x^*)\) (fixed) Individual \(Y^*\) (random)
Extra variance — \(+\;\sigma^2\) for \(\varepsilon^*\)
Formula \(\widehat{Y}^* \pm t\left[\text{MSE}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{n}+\tfrac{(x^*-\bar{X})^2}{S_{xx}}\right)\right]^{1/2}\) \(\widehat{Y}^* \pm t\left[\text{MSE}\!\left(1+\tfrac{1}{n}+\tfrac{(x^*-\bar{X})^2}{S_{xx}}\right)\right]^{1/2}\)
Width as \(n\to\infty\) \(\to 0\) \(\to 2t\,\text{MSE}^{1/2}\)
Narrowest at \(x^* = \bar{X}\) \(x^* = \bar{X}\)
R argument interval = "confidence" interval = "prediction"

A prediction interval is always wider than a confidence interval at the same \(x^*\).

Visual: CI vs. PI

Both bands are narrowest near \(\bar{X} \approx 3.2\) and widen with extrapolation.

CI and PI in R

fit <- lm(mpg ~ wt, data = mtcars)

# 95% CI: average mpg for all cars weighing 3500 lbs
predict(fit, newdata = data.frame(wt = 3.5),
        interval = "confidence", level = 0.95)
       fit      lwr      upr
1 18.57948 17.43342 19.72553
# 95% PI: mpg for one new specific car weighing 3500 lbs
predict(fit, newdata = data.frame(wt = 3.5),
        interval = "prediction", level = 0.95)
       fit      lwr      upr
1 18.57948 12.25426 24.90469

The fit (fit) is identical. The PI is wider because the only change is adding individual-level noise.

Summary

  • CI for mean response at \(x^*\): targets \(E(Y \mid X = x^*)\); uncertainty comes only from estimating \(\widehat{Y}^*\)
  • PI for new observation at \(x^*\): targets an individual \(Y^*\); adds \(\sigma^2\) for irreducible noise — always wider than the CI
  • Both intervals are centered at \(\widehat{Y}^*\) and are narrowest at \(x^* = \bar{X}\), widening with extrapolation
  • PI does not shrink to zero as \(n \to \infty\) — individual noise cannot be eliminated with more data
  • In R: predict(..., interval = "confidence") vs. predict(..., interval = "prediction")
